# DTCO strategy at 28 nm

This document maps the block-shrink strategies to what the model implements, what
it predicts for GRX930 and G100, and what still has to be proven with real
EDA and silicon. Every number is generated by `python -m gpdk` and stored in
[`out/REPORT.md`](../out/REPORT.md) and `out/dtco_all.csv`.

The 12 nm FinFET node definition is retained as the advanced-node reference in
[`out/REPORT.md`](../out/REPORT.md) section 1 and
[`node-definition.md`](node-definition.md). It is not the production target.

## Method

The waterfall starts from **GRX28's** 9T, single-finger, double-diffusion-break
library and changes one decision at a time. Each step is evaluated at the chip's
target clock — 25 MHz for GRX930 (the measured legacy clock, not a node limit)
and 0.75 GHz for G100. If a step would miss the target with its base finger
count, fingers are added up to the cell limit. Density bought by slowing the
block down is not counted, and power is reported at the target clock rather than
at the frequency the library could reach.

Block area is `cell area / routable utilisation + SRAM + IO`, where routable
utilisation is derived from usable M2 tracks per cell and pins per cell. That is
why cell-level savings and block-level savings differ. SRAM area includes macro
periphery (array efficiency 0.72), not just the bitcell array.

### GRX930 is measured, and pad-limited

GRX930's baseline is silicon, not a plan: 46.2 mm² of logic and 40.2 mm² of SRAM
on a 165 mm² die. That implies 34.2 µm² per NAND2-equivalent and 51.1 µm² per
SRAM bit — a legacy node, roughly two orders of magnitude less dense than GRX28.
The same netlist on GRX28 is 0.88 mm² at the 9T baseline (a **98× node jump**) and
0.80 mm² after the levers. Logic falls 81×; SRAM falls 273×.

Its interface, though, is 118 signals plus 60 supply and analog pads: **178 pads,
13.0 mm², a TFBGA-196**. The die is 93.9% empty either way. **For GRX930 the
library ladder is not a die-area lever at all**; it is a power and
integration-headroom lever.

The interface is the real lever. Narrowing the measured 64-bit, 25 MHz bus to a
16-bit DDR port drops the die from 13.0 to 5.1 mm² (7.8 mm² saved, QFN-128).
Integrating the 64 KB main memory, which costs 0.09 mm² on-die, drops it to the
4.0 mm² assembly floor (QFN-64). The whole 9T-to-7.5T ladder is worth 0.08 mm² —
about 100× less than deleting the legacy bus.

The pad ring is generated from the interface plan: 178 pads in two staggered
rows at 152.9 µm minimum pitch, 44/44/45/45 per side, and a 196-ball TFBGA map
with 18 spare balls. The figure and net-level CSVs are in
[`out/grx930_package.png`](../out/grx930_package.png) and section 7 of the
report.

## Lever 1 — Track height

**Mechanism:** cell height = tracks × track pitch. At 28 nm the pitch is 90 nm,
so 9T is 810 nm and 7.5T is 675 nm.

**Result:**

| Step | GRX930 | G100 |
|---|---|---|
| 9T → 7.5T, same single-finger cells | −6.9% block area | −6.6% |
| + MBFF | +0.2% | +0.7% |
| + pin-aware placement | −9.0% cumulative | −8.2% cumulative |

Average cell area falls from 0.4947 µm² (9T) to 0.4122 µm² (7.5T), a 16.7%
reduction. The block gain is smaller because SRAM and IO are unchanged and
because 7.5T drops routable utilisation from 0.78 to 0.70.

**The planar constraint.** There is no single-fin, SDB, CNOD or COAG lever at
28 nm. A 6T library is not modelled: 540 nm rows cannot fit the two width units
a series stack needs, so 7.5T is the density floor. The model therefore has fewer
knobs than the 12 nm study, not more.

## Lever 2 — SRAM and the interface

**Mechanism:** 96 KB of on-chip SRAM at 0.135 µm² per HD bitcell, divided by a
0.72 array efficiency. That is 0.15 mm² of the 0.80 mm² GRX930 content.

**Result:** SRAM is 18% of GRX930 content and 20% of G100 content. At
28 nm it is no longer negligible relative to logic, which is why the SRAM bitcell
and the memory interface matter more than the last 0.02 mm² of cell area.

**Caveat:** the model uses one HD bitcell for all memories. A real design mixes
HD and HC bitcells, and a compiled macro's area depends on aspect ratio, mux
factor and Redundancy. Replace the single number with macro-level areas when the
SRAM compiler is available.

## Lever 3 — BEOL

**Mechanism modelled:** M1/M2 at 90 nm pitch, 45 nm width, with copper
size-effect correction. The 28 nm stack has eight layers and no via pillars.

**Result:** layer RC is reported in section 4 of the report, but BEOL is not in
the 28 nm waterfall. There is no credible area lever here: pitch compression at
this node is a different mask set, not a DTCO knob. The real effect is wire delay
and routability, which the block model captures through the blended M2/M3 RC.

**Caveat:** via pillars are a FinFET-node feature. They are disabled in
`tech/grx28.yaml`; do not carry the 12 nm via-pillar result into a 28 nm plan.

## Lever 4 — Synthesis and place-and-route

**Mechanism modelled:**

- MBFF-2/4 sharing clock and control: 0.90/0.80 area per bit and 0.82/0.65 clock
  capacitance per bit, with a 1.5%/3% routable-utilisation penalty.
- Pin-density-aware placement: 8% more effective pin capacity.

**Result:**

| Step | GRX930 | G100 |
|---|---|---|
| MBFF | area +0.2%, power −0.2 mW | area +0.7%, power −5.0 W |
| Pin-aware placement | area −2.5% | area −2.4% |

MBFF is a **clock-power** lever at these sequential densities (7% and 6% of
gates). The commonly quoted 20–30% saving is for the sequential cells themselves;
at block level, the FF area is too small a fraction to survive the extra
placement congestion. On G100 the area grows slightly while 5 W of clock
power disappears — a good trade, but not a density story.

Pin-density-aware placement is worth about 2.5% of block area. It is cheap to
adopt and should be on by default.

**What the open-source flow adds:** the model cannot predict synthesis quality.
Yosys/ABC area-oriented mapping, `dfflibmap`, `dont_use` on 1-bit flops, and
OpenROAD's resizing and placement iterations are where the next several percent
live. Measure them on the 45 nm proxy before claiming them at 28 nm.

## Recommended configuration

| Chip | Library | Finger policy | MBFF | Placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GRX930 | 7.5T, single-finger, DDB | 1 finger base; upsize only where a path needs drive | 2-bit | pin-aware |
| G100 | 7.5T, single-finger, DDB | 1 finger base, upsize to 2 for the target clock | 4-bit | pin-aware |

For GRX930 the choice is not about die area — 9T and 7.5T land on the same
pad-limited die — but about leakage and integration headroom. For G100,
7.5T is 6.6% smaller and the MBFF-4 + pin-aware combination adds another 1.7%.

## What would change the answer

1. **A real trial route.** If 7.5T cannot close at 0.70 routable utilisation, the
   9T baseline becomes competitive again. This is the largest single risk.
2. **Wire RC after extraction.** The model uses a blended M2/M3 RC. Real
   extraction with coupling, CMP and dummy fill can move long-wire delay by tens
   of percent.
3. **SRAM bitcell and macro area.** SRAM is 18–20% of content. A 10% SRAM shrink
   is worth more than the entire library ladder.
4. **Sequential density.** If the real designs have 15–20% flip-flops instead of
   6–7%, MBFF becomes a genuine area lever and the conclusion reverses.
5. **The GPU clock.** If 1.0 GHz is required, the model already needs two
   fingers at 7.5T; if 0.6 GHz is acceptable, one finger and lower power win.
6. **The package.** At 178 pads the die is fixed at 13.0 mm². A 112-pad DDR
   variant or on-die memory changes the die more than every cell decision
   combined.

## Validation plan

| Stage | Open-source tool | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Synthesis | Yosys + ABC | Gate counts, cell mix, MBFF merging, area at target clock |
| Place and route | OpenROAD (`gpl`, `dpl`, `cts`, `grt`, `drt`, `rsz`) | Utilisation, pin access, DRC closure, 7.5T trial route |
| Extraction | OpenRCX | M1–M3 RC, coupling, via resistance, fill impact |
| STA | OpenSTA | FO4 correlation, clock skew, hold corners |
| Power | OpenROAD + SPICE | Clock power, leakage at 85 °C, IR drop |
| Physical verification | KLayout, Magic, Netgen | DRC, LVS, antenna, density, XOR |
| Silicon | test chip | Ring-oscillator FO4, SRAM margin, thermal, aging |

The 45 nm proxy validates the flow and the relative deltas. Only the NDA 28 nm
kit validates the absolute numbers. See
[`open-source-28nm-flow.md`](open-source-28nm-flow.md).
